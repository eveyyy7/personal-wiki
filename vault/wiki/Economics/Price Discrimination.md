# Price Discrimination

> Charging different markups over cost to different buyer groups.

## Summary

Price discrimination adds a different markup over cost to different buyers; price differences that only reflect cost differences do not count. Third degree prices by segment and first degree by individual. It works only if the seller can identify types, separate them with a fence, and prevent arbitrage.

## Key points

- Price differences that merely reflect cost differences are not price discrimination: a senior movie ticket is pure discrimination, while a business-class seat is mostly a cost difference. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- Price discrimination is adding a different markup over cost to different buyers. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- Third degree pricing involves one price within a group, while first degree is individualized with perfect insight into willingness to pay. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- Three conditions for price discrimination are: ability to identify types, ability to separate them, and no arbitrage. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- The optimal price depends on the customer's level of willingness to pay and their price responsiveness. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
