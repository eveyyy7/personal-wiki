# Two-Part Tariff

> A per-unit price at marginal cost plus a fixed fee equal to consumer surplus.

## Summary

A single monopoly price leaves value uncreated (the deadweight loss) and uncaptured (consumer surplus); the two-part tariff fixes both. The optimal version sets the per-unit price equal to marginal cost and the fixed fee equal to the entire consumer surplus at that price. Its limits are arbitrage, uncertainty about the fee, and customer heterogeneity, which push firms toward price discrimination.

## Key points

- The optimal two-part tariff sets the per-unit price equal to marginal cost and the fixed fee equal to the entire consumer surplus at that price. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- The unit price buys the efficient quantity; the fee captures the surplus. Disney, with zero marginal cost, makes rides free and charges at the gate. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- County Fair example: demand P equals forty minus two q, marginal cost eight, so the unit price is eight, quantity sixteen, and the fee two hundred fifty-six; total three hundred eighty-four versus one hundred twenty-eight from the best single price. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- If marginal cost rises, or demand shifts down in parallel, the consumer-surplus triangle shrinks and the fee falls. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
- Limits: arbitrage (reselling), uncertainty (overshoot the fee and you lose the market), and heterogeneity (one fee cannot fit different customer types). *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 7 — Lecture six: two-part tariff and price discrimination)*
