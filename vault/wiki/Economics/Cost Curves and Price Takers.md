# Cost Curves and Price Takers

> Determining optimal production levels for price takers.

## Summary

A price taker maximizes profit by producing until marginal cost equals price, and shuts down if this results in a loss. Profit is positive when price exceeds average cost, and the lowest price for positive profit is the minimum of average cost. Market supply is the horizontal sum of individual firms' marginal cost curves.

## Key points

- A price taker maximizes profit by producing until marginal cost rises to price and shuts down if doing that loses money. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 4 — Lecture three: cost curves and the price taker)*
- Profit is positive whenever price is above average cost, which is the range between the two quantities where the average cost curve crosses that price. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 4 — Lecture three: cost curves and the price taker)*
- If fixed cost is avoidable, the firm needs price at or above minimum average cost to avoid shutting down. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 4 — Lecture three: cost curves and the price taker)*
- The average-cost fallacy is turning away an order or shutting down when price is below average cost but above avoidable cost. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 4 — Lecture three: cost curves and the price taker)*
- Market supply is the horizontal sum of firms' marginal cost curves. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 4 — Lecture three: cost curves and the price taker)*
