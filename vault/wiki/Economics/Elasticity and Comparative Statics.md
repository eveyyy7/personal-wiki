# Elasticity and Comparative Statics

> Covers demand, elasticity, and comparative statics in market analysis.

## Summary

Market demand is the horizontal sum of quantities at a given price, while shifts are caused by factors other than the good's own price. Elasticity measures the percentage change in quantity relative to the percentage change in price. The incidence rule suggests a shock lands on price when the other curve is inelastic and on quantity when it is elastic.

## Key points

- Market demand is the horizontal sum: at a given price you add quantities, never prices. A change in the good's own price moves you along the curve. Anything else — substitutes, complements, income, quality, expectations — shifts the curve. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 5 — Lecture four: demand, elasticity, and comparative statics)*
- Price up and quantity up means demand shifted out. Price up and quantity down means supply shifted in. Price down and quantity up means supply shifted out. Price down and quantity down means demand shifted in. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 5 — Lecture four: demand, elasticity, and comparative statics)*
- Elasticity equals percentage change in quantity divided by percentage change in price. Absolute value above one is elastic, below one is inelastic. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 5 — Lecture four: demand, elasticity, and comparative statics)*
- A shock lands on price when the other curve is steep — inelastic — and on quantity when the other curve is flat — elastic. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 5 — Lecture four: demand, elasticity, and comparative statics)*
- The more inelastic demand is, the more of a cost increase sellers can pass on to buyers. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 5 — Lecture four: demand, elasticity, and comparative statics)*
