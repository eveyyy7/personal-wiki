# Economic Cost

> Defines economic cost using expenditure, sunk cost, and opportunity cost.

## Summary

Economic cost is calculated by taking expenditure, subtracting sunk cost, and adding opportunity cost. Opportunity cost represents the value of the best alternative foregone, while sunk costs are unchangeable and irrelevant to future decisions. The source details several applications, such as option value and inventory costing.

## Key points

- Economic cost equals expenditure, minus sunk cost, plus opportunity cost. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 3 — Lecture two: economic cost)*
- Opportunity cost is the value of the most highly valued alternative foregone. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 3 — Lecture two: economic cost)*
- Sunk means already spent and unchangeable, so it is irrelevant to any forward-looking decision. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 3 — Lecture two: economic cost)*
- When giving a unit internally at full capacity, charge the external price; if there is slack capacity, charge marginal cost. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 3 — Lecture two: economic cost)*
- Confusing accounting profit with economic profit can lead to destroying value even if a profit is reported. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 3 — Lecture two: economic cost)*
