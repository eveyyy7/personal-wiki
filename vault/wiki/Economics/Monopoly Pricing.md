# Monopoly Pricing

> Monopolist profit maximization rules and pricing formulas.

## Summary

A price maker sets marginal revenue equal to marginal cost to determine profit-maximizing output. The price is then read from the demand curve, ensuring the price remains above marginal cost. Profit calculation involves price minus marginal cost times quantity, minus fixed cost.

## Key points

- A price maker sets marginal revenue equal to marginal cost, and then reads the price off the demand curve. Price stays above marginal cost. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 6 — Lecture five: monopoly, the price maker)*
- Marginal revenue equals price plus the slope times quantity — the gain on the new unit minus the price cut on everything before it. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 6 — Lecture five: monopoly, the price maker)*
- The markup formula is price equals marginal cost divided by one plus one over epsilon. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 6 — Lecture five: monopoly, the price maker)*
- A price taker faces infinitely elastic demand, so price equals marginal cost. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 6 — Lecture five: monopoly, the price maker)*
- Only demand and cost limit a monopolist's profit; having a competitor shifts and flattens the demand curve. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 6 — Lecture five: monopoly, the price maker)*
