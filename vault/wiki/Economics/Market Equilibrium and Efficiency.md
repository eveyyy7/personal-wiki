# Market Equilibrium and Efficiency

> Defines market equilibrium, efficiency, and the role of price.

## Summary

Market demand is the sorted unit value of every buyer, and market supply is the sorted unit cost of every seller. Equilibrium is the price where both excess demand and excess supply disappear. The outcome is efficient in three ways: right quantity, right producers, and right consumers.

## Key points

- Market demand is every buyer's unit value, sorted from high to low. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 2 — Lecture one: equilibrium, and why it is efficient)*
- Market supply is every seller's unit cost, sorted from low to high. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 2 — Lecture one: equilibrium, and why it is efficient)*
- Equilibrium is the price where excess demand and excess supply both vanish. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 2 — Lecture one: equilibrium, and why it is efficient)*
- Efficient means the market creates as much value as it can, ensuring right quantity, right producers, and right consumers. *(source: [[podcast-201a-econ-review]] · Chapter 2 — Lecture one: equilibrium, and why it is efficient)*
